Questions that help you avoid surprises

A common mistake is to describe only the result, such as a room feeling uncomfortable, without mentioning when it happens or what has changed. Another is to assume the first suggested solution is the only option. Share the details you have, and ask for an explanation before agreeing to work.

Keep these questions nearby:
- Is this a heating issue, a cooling issue, or could it involve both?
- What will you check to understand the concern?
- What does the recommended work include?
- Are there other options I should consider?
- Is there anything I should do before the appointment?

You don’t need to diagnose the equipment yourself. Don’t open or adjust unfamiliar components just to gather more information. A clear description of the symptoms is a better starting point than a guess about what has failed.
